My path into chemical biology

I began my scientific training at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Kumasi, Ghana, graduating in the top 1% of my chemistry class. In the Borquaye Research Group, I worked across natural-products isolation, antimicrobial testing, small-molecule synthesis, and computational studies of ligand–protein interactions.

As a laboratory technician at KNUST’s Central Laboratory, I supported researchers from across Africa using NMR, GC-MS, HPLC, UV spectroscopy, and atomic absorption spectroscopy. That experience strengthened my appreciation for careful measurement and for the role that shared scientific infrastructure plays in enabling discovery.

At the University of Southern California, I joined Professor Matthew Pratt’s laboratory to pursue a Ph.D. in Chemistry. My graduate research integrates recombinant protein production, solid-phase peptide synthesis, protein semisynthesis, biophysical characterization, and cell-based assays. Across distinct projects, one question connects my work: how can chemical precision reveal the ways protein sequence and post-translational modification control biological behavior?

I am especially interested in expanding this foundation toward chemical proteomics, mass spectrometry, and translational drug discovery.
